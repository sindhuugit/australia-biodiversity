CONSERVATION PROGRAM 01 / 02 / THE GREAT BARRIER REEF

A Fight for the Reef

Stretching more than 2,300 kilometres along Queensland's coast, the Great Barrier Reef supports extraordinary marine life. But one native predator can become a serious threat when its numbers explode: the coral-eating crown-of-thorns starfish.

Since 2012, the Crown-of-thorns Starfish Control Program has deployed specialist dive teams to survey reefs, detect outbreaks and remove starfish from priority locations. Explore the scale of this work, a documented control case study and historical monitoring patterns.

03 — THE CONSERVATION RESPONSE

Protecting What Remains, Restoring What Was Lost

Koala conservation requires both protecting existing habitat and repairing degraded landscapes. Of the A$102.1 million across four selected NSW Koala Strategy allocations, A$70.6 million supports habitat protection and A$31.5 million supports habitat restoration.

These are announced allocations under the broader 2021–26 strategy, not spending exclusively on recovery from the 2019–20 bushfires.

WHAT THE INVESTMENT SUPPORTS

Four approaches to koala habitat conservation

Protection secures habitat already in place. Restoration aims to improve habitat condition and connectivity over time. Area figures are targets, not verified completed results.

01 · PROTECT
National park additions

Bring more suitable koala habitat into protected areas to secure important landscapes over time.

A$50.3m · 15,000 ha target
02 · CONSERVE
Private land protection

Conserve koala habitat on privately owned land, including areas outside national parks.

A$20.3m · 7,000 ha target
03 · RESTORE
Box-gum woodland restoration

Support native woodland restoration and improve ecological condition in landscapes important to wildlife.

A$16.0m · 5,000 ha target
04 · RECONNECT
Coordinated habitat restoration

Support landscape-scale projects that can improve habitat quality and links between natural areas.

A$15.5m · 20,000 ha target

Why this matters after the fires: Retaining unburnt habitat and restoring damaged landscapes can complement bushfire recovery, although these allocations address koala conservation more broadly.

Key Finding: About 69% of the funding shown goes to habitat protection and 31% to restoration. National park additions receive almost half of the selected total. These are funding plans, not evidence that all habitat targets have been achieved or that koala populations have recovered.
