Australia's Biodiversity

Exploring Australia's unique wildlife, threatened species and conservation efforts.

By Sindhu Modugu

How Much of Australia Is Protected?

Australia's protected areas play an important role in conserving natural habitats and ecosystems. However, the proportion of protected land varies significantly across states and territories.

Hover over a state or territory to explore its protected land coverage.

Key Finding: The ACT has the highest percentage of protected land at 55.9%, followed by Tasmania at 42.8%. Queensland has the lowest among the eight main jurisdictions, at 9.4%.

Source: CAPAD 2024, Australian Government. Geographic boundaries: ABS ASGS 2021.

Australia's Disappearing Wildlife

Australia is home to an extraordinary range of native species, but many face increasing threats to their survival. The Australian Threatened Species Index tracks changes in the relative abundance of monitored threatened species over time.

Select a species group using the dropdown menu to explore how its index has changed between 1985 and 2022. Hover over the line to see the index for a particular year.

The index is relative to 1985 (1985 = 1). A value of 0.5 indicates an estimated abundance half that of the reference year for the monitored species represented in the index. The shaded area shows published uncertainty bounds for the official TSX groups.

Key Finding: The overall threatened species index declined from 1.00 in 1985 to approximately 0.25 in 2022, representing a decrease of around 75%. This indicates a substantial decline in the relative abundance of monitored threatened species.

Source: Australian Threatened Species Index 2025. Data period: 1985–2022.

Beyond the Numbers: Exploring Threatened Species

Australia's threatened wildlife includes an extraordinary diversity of birds, mammals, plants, reptiles and amphibians. While the overall Threatened Species Index reveals long-term changes, each group has its own story to tell.

Select a species group below to explore its data.

Threat Levels Across Species Groups

Explore the distribution of recorded threat categories among monitored species groups.

Reptiles & Amphibians (76.9%) and Plants (72.5%) have the highest proportions of Endangered or Critically Endangered species in the monitoring dataset, suggesting that these groups face particularly severe extinction risks. In contrast, Mammals have the largest proportion classified as Vulnerable (40.2%). This highlights how the severity of threats differs across species groups, rather than affecting all wildlife equally.

Source: Australian Threatened Species Index 2025. Counts of distinct species in the monitoring extract.
